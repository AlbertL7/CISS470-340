01

One campus. One connected story.

Start with what matters to the organization. Follow it all the way to a verified setting.

Notes: explain the whole connection in two minutes

You could say: “Imagine a staff member leaves a workstation unlocked with campus records open. Before we change a setting, we need to understand what we are protecting and what harm could occur. FIPS 199 helps us categorize that harm. FIPS 200 sets minimum protection requirements. NIST's control catalog and baselines help us choose safeguards. An applicable DISA STIG gives us technical requirements, checks, and fixes for the product. SP 800-70 explains how to use and maintain configuration checklists responsibly. Finally, we verify what happened and report the remaining risk to the people responsible.”

NIST is the National Institute of Standards and Technology. FIPS means Federal Information Processing Standards. SP means Special Publication. NIST and DISA are organizations; FIPS publications, NIST Special Publications, and STIGs are different kinds of guidance they produce.

Each document answers a different question.

FIPS 199
How harmful would loss of confidentiality, integrity, or availability be?What we carry forward: the three impact ratings and the reasons for them.
FIPS 200
Which minimum protection areas must we address?What we carry forward: requirements across 17 areas and impact-based control selection.
SP 800-53 / 53B
Which safeguards should protect this system?800-53 defines controls; 800-53B supplies starting baselines and tailoring guidance.
DISA STIG
How should this particular technology be configured and checked?What we carry forward: applicable technical requirements, check procedures, and fix guidance.
SP 800-70
How do we develop, select, use, and maintain dependable checklists?This supports the checklist lifecycle around the technical work.

Governance connects the beginning and end: leaders establish priorities and responsibility, then use the evidence to decide what still needs attention. This is a teaching map, not a rule that every organization must follow all these documents in this exact order. Our campus is an analogy; federal and DoD applicability still matters.

02

How much harm could a loss cause?

FIPS 199 separates three questions. Low means limited harm, Moderate means serious harm, and High means severe or catastrophic harm.

👁

Someone sees records they should not see. How harmful could disclosure be?

✎

Someone changes the records without permission. How harmful could that change be?

⏱

People cannot use an essential service. How harmful could the interruption be?

Illustrative overall system impact

Moderate

C: Moderate · I: Moderate · A: Low. Start with a Moderate security baseline.

Use the highest impact, not an average. FIPS 199 preserves the separate CIA ratings. FIPS 200 uses the highest objective to determine the overall system impact and initial security baseline.

This is a simplified illustration of the federal non-national-security model. The example ratings are assumptions to discuss, not fixed ratings for every payroll or service system. Real categorization considers all information types and system context.

Impact and likelihood answer different questions.

Impact: how bad would the consequences be? Likelihood: how plausible is the event in this environment? FIPS 199 categorization is not a complete risk assessment. A low chance of an event does not make its consequences small.

Notes: walk through the ratings and the highest-impact rule

Give each rating a reason.

Continue with the records on our campus workstation. For this fictional example, assume:

  • Confidentiality = Moderate: disclosure of the private records could cause serious harm.
  • Integrity = Moderate: unauthorized changes could lead to seriously incorrect decisions or payments.
  • Availability = Low: a brief interruption has limited consequences because a tested workaround is available in this scenario.

Keep C = Moderate, I = Moderate, A = Low in the record. Under the simplified FIPS 199/200 model, the highest of those ratings makes the overall system impact Moderate. These are assumptions for discussion, not an official categorization of campus records.

You could say: “Two limited consequences do not cancel out one catastrophic consequence. That is why we use the highest impact rather than averaging the three.”

Try it: the payroll preset uses the same Moderate/Moderate/Low pattern. Then choose the critical-service example, where an outage has High impact. Ask what changed about the business consequences. The button is doing arithmetic; the hard part is justifying the inputs with the system owner.

Know what applies. FIPS 199/200 have a defined federal scope. STIG obligations depend on the system, DoD requirements, agency policy, or contract. A business can also adopt them voluntarily. A guide's existence does not make every rule mandatory for every organization.

Notes: distinguish a policy, a control, a baseline, and a setting

Follow one protection from broad to specific.

  1. Organizational policy: “Protect private campus records and prevent unauthorized use of unattended sessions.” This states the organization's direction and responsibilities.
  2. Security control: a safeguard that addresses a protection need. For example, SP 800-53 AC-11, Device Lock, addresses locking access and requiring authentication to regain access. Controls also include training, procedures, planning, and other work beyond computer settings.
  3. Technical implementation: for our example, configure automatic inactivity locking and authentication on resume. AC-11 allows defined choices about automatic and/or user-initiated locking; it does not prescribe one universal timeout. Use the values required by the applicable policy and actual technical rule.
  4. Verification: follow the applicable check to determine whether the effective setting and required behavior meet the requirement.

This illustrates a relationship to AC-11, not a verified mapping to a particular STIG rule. The simulator deliberately uses a fictional ID and no timeout or CAT assignment.

Why both 800-53 and 800-53B?

A catalog describes available controls. A baseline is a starting set appropriate to an impact level. Think of a building supplier's full catalog versus a starting protection plan for a particular building. Tailoring adjusts that starting set to the system's context using documented, approved reasoning. It does not mean removing difficult requirements without justification.

Where does 800-70 go?

It supports the process around the checklist: choosing one that fits, reviewing and testing it, applying authorized changes, recording results, and keeping it current. A checklist for a different product or version can lead to the wrong conclusion. A STIG also does not replace the entire security program or every control in the catalog.

Notes: read a STIG finding and explain why the recheck matters

A STIG contains requirements; a finding records an assessment result. An assessment checklist helps track what was checked, what was observed, and what remains unresolved. Before fixing anything, read these parts of the actual rule:

  • Identifier and title: which requirement and target are we discussing?
  • Discussion or rationale: what weakness is the requirement addressing?
  • Check procedure: what do we inspect, and what result satisfies the requirement?
  • Fix guidance: what authorized change can correct the problem?
  • Severity and status: how serious is this requirement or finding, and what did our assessment conclude?

During the activity: “Inspect tells us what is wrong. Apply fix tells us what we changed. Recheck tells us whether the change actually produced the required result. Those are three different claims.”

A selected checkbox can show a configured value without proving it is effective. For example, a higher-priority policy might override a local setting. Evidence must satisfy the real check, including effective policy or observed behavior where required. A screenshot should identify the relevant target and result, not merely show that a settings window was open.

Ask the class: “We applied the change. Why is the finding still Open?” Answer: because we have not yet verified that the requirement is satisfied. The browser's recheck is a worked simulation; Wednesday's lab needs observations from the actual target.

Transition: “Once we have evidence, what can we tell the people responsible for the risk?”

05

Finding severity is not system impact.

FIPS describes the consequences of compromise to information and systems. A STIG category describes the severity assigned to an individual requirement or finding.

CAT IHigh

Highest finding severity. Give it prompt attention and assess exposure and consequences.

CAT IIMedium

A meaningful weakness that still needs treatment. Medium does not mean optional.

CAT IIILow

A weakness in protective measures. Track and address it within the risk-management process.

No automatic conversion: a High-impact system is not “a CAT I system.” A CAT II finding does not make a system Moderate impact. Prioritization also considers exposure, exploitability, important services, and dependencies.

What if the fix breaks something important?

Document the finding, the operational effect, and possible alternative protections. Escalate through the applicable risk and change process. The accountable authority decides whether to accept residual risk.

An approved exception does not erase a failed technical check. Not Applicable needs a real applicability reason, not inconvenience. In this lab, work toward all required checks passing.

Notes: connect a technical finding back to business risk

Keep three questions separate.

  • System impact: how harmful could loss of confidentiality, integrity, or availability be?
  • Finding severity: how serious is this individual requirement or weakness according to its assigned STIG category?
  • Assessment status: has it been reviewed, and does the evidence show the requirement is satisfied?

Passing two checks does not make a Moderate-impact system Low-impact. A CAT II finding does not make it Moderate-impact either. Those labels answer different questions.

In our example, the weakness is an accessible unattended session. Someone could use it to view or change records without authorization. Locking the session reduces that opportunity. Residual risk is the risk that remains after protections are applied; this one fix does not address every way records might be exposed or changed.

You could say: “If 90 out of 100 checks pass, we have a useful assessment result. We do not know that the system is 90 percent secure. We still need to understand the ten remaining findings, the scope of the checks, and the consequences.”

Connect to Antonucci: ownership tells us who must act; policy sets expectations; people and processes make the changes dependable; meaningful measurement explains whether important risks are being treated. Report the affected service, evidence, unresolved findings, responsible owner, and next review. An approved risk exception records a decision; it does not change a failing technical result into a pass.

06

Bring the whole story into CyberRange.

For each finding, be able to explain the business risk, the required protection, and the evidence that the protection works.

Notes: a simple class plan and answers to likely questions

A suggested 25-minute review: spend 3 minutes on the campus story, 5 on the document connections, 4 on the CIA activity, 7 on inspecting/fixing/rechecking, and 6 on the quiz and Wednesday's expectations. Use the notes you need; you do not have to read every paragraph aloud.

“Why do we need a control catalog and a STIG?”
The catalog describes safeguards and their requirements. Applicable STIG guidance supplies technology-specific requirements, checks, and fixes. One configured setting supports part of the protection, not the entire program.
“Is SP 800-70 another checklist of Windows settings?”
No. It explains the development and use of security configuration checklists. The applicable product checklist supplies the specific technical requirements.
“Does Moderate impact mean CAT II findings?”
No. Impact describes potential harm to information and systems. A CAT label describes an individual requirement or finding's severity.
“Can I just show the checkbox after changing it?”
Only if that evidence satisfies the applicable check. Check effective policy and behavior when required, and record the actual target and result.
“What if a fix interrupts an important service?”
Document the finding and operational effect, investigate alternatives, and follow the authorized change and risk process. Approval to accept risk does not mean the technical check passed.

Close with: “On Wednesday, every change needs a reason and a result. Tell me what was at risk, what the requirement called for, what you changed, and what evidence shows it worked. That is how the technical lab connects to governance.”
